  - You can go through all the steps with varying complexity of forms

** Phase 1 (pyramid base)
*** Step 1 - Become intuitive with perspective
    - Be able to draw forms in perspective without guidelines
    - Believability over strict, technical accuracy
**** Exercises
     - draw basic forms in believable perspective without a grid
     - draw a scene of basics forms in believable perspective without a grid
     - exercises from dynamic bible and dynamic sketching
     - recede same-sized planes in space
     - 4 and 5 point basic forms
     - 4 and 5 point scenes of basic forms
*** Step 2 - Form Manipulation
    - modifications start simple and become progressively closer to what you are
      studying or ultimately trying to draw
    - draw through until you don't need to
    - based on having intuitive perspective
**** Exercises
     - for each basic form primitive
       - curve
       - stretch
       - squash
       - modify orthographic view
       - modify contour (top view)
       - modify edges (rounding or bevel), single or double
       - form addition or combination
       - form extraction
       - form subtraction (boolean other forms)
       - taper
*** Step 3 - Proportions
    - combine forms focusing on getting proportions right
    - start with simple, hard surface objects
**** Exercises
     - draw simple hard surface objects with corrct proportion
       - first understand the underlying forms (basic or manipulated)
       - combine the forms in the correct proportions
       - rotate them in space
     - try varying proportions (big, medium, small) to come up with interesting designs
     - mannequinize figures, focusing on proportion
       - rotate them in space
*** Step 4 - Study what looks good
    - keeps you motivated
    - checks your learning
    - copy artists' works
    - not a line-for-line copy. Understand the manipulated forms and reconstruct them
**** Exercises
     - start by deconstructing the objects into simple, then more complex manipulated forms
     - reconstruct the drawing by putting them together
     - understand the forms
     - add details
*** Step 5 - Reverse Engineering
    - taking random scribble shapes and turning them into recognizable forms with structure
    - turning a 2D shape into a 3D form
**** Exercises
     - draw a random 2D shape
     - sculpt it into a recognizable 3D form
** Phase 2 (pyramid middle)
*** Step 6 - Indicate form with shadow
    - go back to basic and manipulated forms
    - indicate the form by hatching along cross contour lines
    - can use the same strategy with thicker strokes when painting
**** Exercises
     - shade basic and manipulated forms with cross contour hatching
*** Step 7 - Cell shading
    - simplify into light side and shadow side
    - graphically indicate shadows
**** Exercises
     - cell shade basic forms
     - just 2 values
     - rotate form and light source
*** Step 8 - Cell shading on complex forms
**** Exercises
     - shade complex, manipulated forms from step 2
     - 2 values
     - rotate form and light source
*** Step 9 - Cell shading on believable forms
    - loomis heads
    - mannequinized figures
    - manipulated forms that resemble source something to be believable
**** Exercises
     - different lighting situations for believable
       - loomis heads
       - mannequins
       - hard surface
       - really any subject
*** Step 10 - Study what looks good
    - construct from other artists like step 4, but now add value
    - how do they use shape design?
    - how do they use hatching?
    - how do they turn form with shadow?
**** Exercises
     - master studies like step 4, but focusing on how value reveals 3D form
*** Step 11 - Reverse Engineering
    - same as step 5, but add shadow
**** Exercises
     - reverse engineering exercise
     - add cell shading
     - add hatching or contour
     - design cool shadow shapes that also reveal the form
** Phase 3 (pyramid top)
*** Step 12 - Half tones
    - start with simple forms
    - add cell shading
    - add half tone values to describe the form in the light
    - still greyscale
**** Exercises
     - step 7 (simple forms) but add half tones
       - hatching
       - gouache
       - markers
       - oil
     - step 8 (complex forms) with half tones
     - step 9 (believable forms) with half tones
*** Step 13 - Color
    - start with limited palette and gradually increase
    - monochromatic
      - one color plus black and white
    - monochromatic plus opposite on color wheel
    - Zorn
    - color wheel triangle gamut
    - warm cool
      - cool shadows
      - warm light
    - unlimited
    - value is still king
**** Exercises
     - with each palette
       - add color to basic, complex, and believable forms
*** Step 14 - Study what looks good
    - How are your favorite artists using half tones and color?
**** Exercises
     - master studies like step 10, but add half tones
     - masters tudies like step 10, but add color
*** Step 15 - Emulate
    - Create something new in the style of art that exists
    - Emulating a master without knowing their process is a sign of a good artist
    - Using understanding and fundamentals instead of just copying
**** Exercises
     - draw an original subject in the style that some artist draws that subject
     - draw an original subject in the style that some artist draws a different subject
     - E.g., paint a goblin in the style of Leyendecker
*** Step 16 - Experiment
    - This is the end goal
    - Create art from imagination
    - Fail often, but do it a lot
**** Exercises
     - illustration process
     - concept design process

* Studying Subjects
   - Study a single subject intensely for a week or two at a time
   - This could be something of interest, or your current biggest weakness
   - Use the 16 step, 3 tiered pyramid above
** Process
   - Start from life
   - break down into simplest forms
   - get the proportion right
   - turn those around in space
   - light them
   - do them from memory
   - break down into more complex forms
   - get the proportion right
   - turn in space
   - light them
   - do them from memory
   - break down into final forms
   - get the proportion right
   - turn in space
   - light them
   - details and rendering
   - do them from memory
   - learn about them (anatomy, education, function, etc.)
   - repeat process with favorite artists instead of from life
   - create original final redesigns using illustration process
     - reverse engineering
     - thumbnails
     - sketches
     - value studies
     - color comps
     - final

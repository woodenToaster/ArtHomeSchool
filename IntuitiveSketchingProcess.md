
## Phase 1 - Linear
The goal of this phase is intuitive perspective. That is, being able to draw
simple to complex forms in perspective without guidelines or a grid. We are
trying to achieve something that's believable rather than something that is 100%
technically accurate. This phase is line only (no value, color, or lighting),
with the ideal tool being a fine liner pen.
### Task 1 - 1 page of simple form rotations from reference
    * Gather photo reference (no art yet) of your subject
    * Break down the subject of study into the simplest possible form
    * Use only basic forms for now (box, cylinder, pyramid, cone, sphere) with simple modifications (stretch, bend, add, subtract)
    * Try not to use a grid, and work towards minimal construction lines
    * Use contour lines to better describe the form
    * Once you have a form to describe your simplified subject, rotate it around in space, drawing it from multiple points of view
### Task 2 - 1 page of simple form rotations from memory
    * Using the simplified form from task 1, draw it again from multiple points of view, but this time do it from memory without looking at any reference.
    * For an added challenge, try to make all the forms part of a single scene by having a common horizon line.
### Task 3 - 1 page of complex form rotations with correct proportions
    * Manipulate your simple form from task 1 into something more complex that better describes the real subject
    * Possible manipulations: curve, stretch, squash, contour manipulation, edge manipulation (bevel), addition, subtraction, extraction, taper.
    * Focus on getting the proportions correct. Draw orthographic views if necessary, then transfer them into perspective.
    * Draw the complex form from multiple points of view, rotating it around in space.
### Task 4 - 1 page of complex form rotations with correct proportions from memory
    * Repeat the previous task, but do it from memory without looking at reference.
### Task 5 - 1 page of believable forms from reference
    * Start with your complex form from task 3
    * Use this as an under drawing, and layer on the final believable subject
    * Here's where you add all the details and minor variations in silhouette, but remember that we're still focusing on line drawings, so don't get into rendering texture and materials yet.
### Task 6 - 1 page of believable forms from imagination
    * We already know how to rotate the complex forms in space, so now we just have to layer the final believable forms on top of that to get our subject from imagination.
    * Try to do this from memory.
### Task 7 - 2 pages of master studies with a focus on how other artists use line and perspective to describe form
    * Find some art that you like that illustrates the subject.
    * You're studying what looks good to you in order to get those shapes into your subconscious mind.
    * Study how the artist uses line to describe form.
    * This isn't just a line-for-line copy. You should construct your froms from the orignal just like you would from photo reference.
    * Take this opportunity to learn about your subject. Understanding the function of what you're drawing can help you recall the form later.
### Task 8 - 1 page of the scribble sculpting exercise
    * I remember doing this as a kid. I would draw a scribbly shape, then hand it to my friend who had to turn it into something recognizable. Then he'd do the same for me.
    * Draw a scribly shape
    * Try to see your subject within that shape
    * Sculpt the scribble into a 3D form representing your subject
## Phase 2 - Two Value (Light and Shadow)
Here we repeat several tasks from phase I, except now we focus on how we can use
light and shadow to describe form. We'll use a dark value for the shadow, and
the blank page for light. If you're more advanced, you can use more shadow
values, but I would keep it to 2-3 max. Greyscale markers or crosshatching with
a fineliner work well for traditional, but digital is also an option.
### Task 1 - 1 page of simple forms with hatching or texture for shadow
    * Take the simple forms from Phase I Task 1
    * Draw them from multiple points of view, in many rotations
    * Use hatching or texturing to add shadow.
    * Vary the direction of the light source.
    * Take this opportunity to study the details, materials, and textures of your subject.
### Task 2 - 1 page of complex forms with single value cell shading
    * Take the complex forms from Phase I Task 3
    * Draw them from multiple points of view, in many rotations
    * Decide on a light direction and fill in the shadow shapes with a single shadow value
    * Design the shape of your shadow to be interesting
### Task 3 - 1 page of believable forms with multiple shadow values
    * Same as Phase I Task 5, but now we're adding realistic shadow value.
    * Use 3 shadow values: bounce light, core shadow, and occlusion shadow
    * Use different viewpoints and different lighting situations
### Task 4 - 2 pages of master studies with a focus on how other artists use light and shadow to describe form
    * Same as Phase I Task 7, but now focusing on light vs. shadow
### Task 5 - 1 page of the scribble sculpting exercise
    * Same as Phase I Task 8, but add shadow values
## Phase 3 - Halftones and Color
We go through the tasks one more time, but now adding halftones, starting with
greyscale (2-3 values in the light), then adding in color.
### Task 1 - 1 page of forms with halftones added
    * Use 2-3 values of halftone (greyscale) in the lights
    * You probably have to fill in the shadows too for it to look right
    * Use whatever complexity of form you're comfortable with
    * If you're having trouble with halftones on believalbe forms, step it back to complex forms
    * The more you can create the forms from memory or imagination, the better
### Task 2 - 1 page of believable forms with color
    * If you can already make believable forms in greyscale, then adding color is simple
    * Start with a monchrome palette (one hue, plus white and black)
    * Add a second color or use the Zorn palette
    * Work towards a warm/cool palette (warm lights and cool shadows)
    * By now you should be able to invent all your forms from imagination, but use reference for color.
### Task 3 - 2 pages of master studies with a focus on how other artists use halftones and color.
    * Same as Phase I Task 5, but now focusing on halftones and color
## Phase 4 - Create Art
We should now have a good enough familiarity with our subject to create some
original, imaginative art. The pieces for this phase can take many forms:
illustration, concept design, comic panel/page. Ideally it should be something
more finished than a sketch, though you might do some preliminary sketches as
prep for the final piece. The pieces should include your subject of study, but
the subject needn't necessarily be the primary focus. Choose any medium you
wish.
### Task 1 - 1 page of master emulation
    * Draw your subject in the style of an artist you like
    * However, make sure your subject is different from the subject in the piece you choose
    * Example: choose a figure painting, and draw a tiger in that style
    * Example: choose a landscape painting and draw a figure in that style
    * Example: draw a car in the style of a Frazetta figure
    * Figure out the essence of how the artist is using line, value, color, shapes, and composition, and try to incorporate that into your piece.
### Task 2 - 2 original pieces of art
    * Create a concept design of your subject.
    * Create an illustration involving your subject.
    * This task might involve thumbnails, color comps, value studies, turnarounds, breakdowns, whatever it takes to get the final piece.


## Phase 1 - Linear
The goal of this phase is intuitive perspective. That is, being able to draw
simple to complex forms in perspective without guidelines or a grid. We are
trying to achieve something that's believable rather than something that is 100%
technically accurate. This phase is line only (no value, color, or lighting),
with the ideal tool being a fine liner pen.
### Task 1 - 1 page of simple form rotations from reference
    * Gather photo reference (no art yet) of your subject
    * Break down the subject of study into the simplest possible form
    * Use only basic forms for now (box, cylinder, pyramid, cone, sphere) with simple modifications (stretch, bend, add, subtract)
    * Try not to use a grid, and work towards minimal construction lines
    * Use contour lines to better describe the form
    * Once you have a form to describe your simplified subject, rotate it around in space, drawing it from multiple points of view
### Task 2 - 1 page of simple form rotations from memory
    * Using the simplified form from task 1, draw it again from multiple points of view, but this time do it from memory without looking at any reference.
    * For an added challenge, try to make all the forms part of a single scene by having a common horizon line.
### Task 3 - 1 page of complex form rotations with correct proportion
    * Manipulate your simple form from task 1 into something more complex that better describes the real subject
    * Possible manipulations: curve, stretch, squash, contour manipulation, edge manipulation (bevel), addition, subtraction, extraction, taper.
    * Focus on getting the proportions correct. Draw orthographic views if necessary, then transfer them into perspective.

## Phase 2 - Two Value (Light and Shadow)
Here we repeat several tasks from phase I, except now we focus on how we can use
light and shadow to describe form. We'll use a dark value for the shadow, and
the blank page for light. If you're more advanced, you can use more shadow
values, but I would keep it to 2-3 max. Greyscale markers or crosshatching with
a fineliner work well for traditional, but digital is also an option.

## Phase 3 - Halftones and Color
We go through the tasks one more time, but now adding halftones, starting with
greyscale (2-3 values in the light), then adding in color. The capstone of this
phase is to produce your original works from imagination, incorporating the subject
of study.

## Phase 4 - Create Art
We should now have a good enough familiarity with our subject to create some
original, imaginative art. The pieces for this phase can take many forms:
illustration, concept design, comic panel/page. Ideally it should be something
more finished than a sketch, though you might do some preliminary sketching as
prep for the final piece. It should include your subject of study, but the
subject needn't necessarily be the primary focus. Choose any medium you wish.
